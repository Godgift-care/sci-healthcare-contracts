pub fn match_voucher(&self, sponsor: &Address, voucher_id: &u64) -> u64

Matches a voucher: the sponsor’s budget pays for a second voucher for the same care.

The matching voucher is created by the voucher contract and funded by this contract, so it is escrowed, claimed, attested and settled like any other. It carries the same beneficiary, the same clinic and the same service as the voucher that was matched, and it expires with it.

The two are linked both ways: get_match takes the voucher that was matched, get_match_of takes the matching voucher.

Errors:

  • RulesNotSet — the sponsor has not set any rules.
  • VoucherNotFound — there is no such voucher.
  • VoucherNotFunded — the voucher is not Funded.
  • VoucherExpired — the voucher has expired.
  • AlreadyMatched — the voucher has already been matched.
  • SelfMatchNotAllowed — the sponsor funded the voucher itself, or this contract did.
  • CountryNotEligible, ServiceNotEligible — the clinic or the service is outside the sponsor’s rules.
  • VoucherTooNew, VoucherTooSmall — the voucher is outside the sponsor’s rules.
  • MatchBelowPrice — the match is worth less than the service, and a voucher cannot be funded below the price of the care it pays for.
  • BudgetExhausted — the sponsor’s budget cannot cover the match.

pub fn reconcile_sponsor(&self, sponsor: &Address)

Brings a sponsor’s books up to date with the matching vouchers that have since settled or been refunded.

A settled match is money that became care: it stays spent. A refunded match is money that came back: it is available again. The desk cannot see a refund happen, so it checks the matching vouchers themselves.

Permissionless: anyone may call it, and match_voucher and withdraw call it for the sponsor they are acting for.

pub fn budget_summary(&self) -> (i128, i128)

Read-only solvency check: (available_budget, token_balance).

available_budget is every sponsor’s unused budget added together. token_balance is this contract’s actual balance of the settlement token. The two are equal when the accounting is right; a balance that is larger means a matching voucher was refunded and the books have not been reconciled yet.

No authorisation: anyone may run the check.
